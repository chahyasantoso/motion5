## Invariant

A compiled timeline's real duration is the authored track duration, whatever the authored stops do at the tail, so `progress(v)` always means the same authored position.

## The defect

GSAP derives a timeline's duration from its latest scheduled child. If the last authored stop sits before `p = 1`, the timeline becomes short, rescales every authored position, and reports the wrong public `InterpolationTimeline.duration`. A stop at `p = 0.5` therefore makes `progress(0.9)` read mid-ramp instead of holding the final value.

## Implementation

Compilation reports the latest authored stop position directly. When the authored end is before `1`, the GSAP adapter schedules one zero-length tween at the authored end on an adapter-owned anchor, never on the published proxy. A complete grid gets no extra tween. The fix stays inside the interpolator adapter and does not add a clock or graph state.
