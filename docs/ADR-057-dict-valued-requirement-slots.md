# ADR-057: A dict-valued `requires` slot is a declared capability, and its key is a field

**Status:** Accepted, 2026-08-30

**Context.** ADR-052 made a solver's goals authorable as a dict keyed by member id, and reached that through two mechanisms rather than one. `contract/solver-slots.ts` held a grammar: `goalSlot(memberId)` built the string `targets[<memberId>]` and `readGoalSlot` parsed it back, so one fact had a builder in one module and a parser in another by construction. `PluginDefinition.claimsSlot` held a predicate, because a record of declared slots cannot enumerate a family whose members are named by the rig, so "is this slot declared" had two owners kept consistent by hand. `readPluginBindings` special-cased exactly `PLUGIN_GOALS_SLOT` ahead of any registry, which made the capability `ik`'s alone: a second plugin wanting a dict-valued slot was an edit to the parser, not a plugin.

Six source files reached that grammar, plus its test. The framing that this is not a cardinality problem was correct: `GraphNode.edges` was already `readonly GraphEdge[]`, and a dict of N distinct sources correctly derived N edges.

**Decision.** The slot name stays exactly as the author wrote it, and the key becomes a field.

`contract/keyframe-shape.ts` owns detection, by shape, with no registry. A record value under any slot expands to one binding per key, sorted, each carrying `slot` as authored and `memberKey` as data. It says nothing about whether the slot was allowed to carry a dict, because it cannot: it holds no registry, and a slot name proves nothing either way.

`PluginRegistry` owns whether it was allowed. `PluginRequirement.dict` is the declaration and `#resolveRequirements` is its one reader. That is the same split ADR-044 draws for keys: the contract layer owns the spelling, the registry owns the declaration, the graph owns resolution. It is data rather than a predicate, so no plugin answers with code, and `requirements` is the single source of "declared" again.

The refusal runs in both directions, because a mismatch is silent in both. `plugin-requirement-dict-unsupported` is a dict at a slot that takes one source: a binding accepted and then ignored, which rule 6 of ADR-033 forbids, and one that nothing below the registry can catch, because the parser holds no registry and the slot name is legitimately declared. `plugin-requirement-dict-required` is one source at a slot that takes a dict: a plugin reading that slot as a keyed record is handed one node's values under the slot itself with nothing to say the author meant otherwise.

`graph/ir.ts` owns identity and resolution. `memberKey` is length-prefixed into `requirementIdentity`, is the last tiebreak in `compareEdges`, and is surfaced in `describeEdge`. `goalBindingsOf` reads the field and gates on the base slot.

`contract/solver-slots.ts` shrinks to one constant. `goalSlot`, `readGoalSlot` and the slot regex are deleted, and so are `PluginSlotClaim`, `PluginDefinition.claimsSlot`, its registration type guard, and the loop that reserved the goal spellings as declared slot names. Declaring `targets` is now how the capability is declared at all.

**Two findings that changed the shape of the work.** Neither was in the issue's proposal, and each would have made the suite red on its own.

Edge identity breaks first. `edgeKey` composes `observerId`, `sourceId`, `role` and `requirementIdentity`, and that identity was length-prefixed plugin and slot only. Two goals naming the *same* source node were distinguished purely by the encoded slot, `targets[a]` against `targets[b]`. Collapse the slot to its base name and both edges become byte-identical, and `finalizeGraph` reports `observation-duplicate` on a rig that loaded clean. The claim that the string encoding was not load-bearing was false in exactly one place, and this was it. `DV-4` pins it, written before the refactor so it is a regression pin rather than a discovery.

The field is not sufficient on its own either. `goalBindingsOf` classifies goal bindings with no registry in reach, so gating on `memberKey !== undefined` alone would read a future spring or spline plugin's dict as IK goals and run six IK rules over it. It gates on `slot === PLUGIN_GOALS_SLOT` as well, which puts `targets` in the same set as the `root`, `solver`, `base` and `target` literals that layer already hardcodes. So the accurate claim about the constant is narrower than "private to `ik`": it stops being a reserved parser constant and becomes the graph layer's IK slot literal, exactly on par with four names already there. `DV-8` pins it.

**The publisher nests rather than documenting a collision as harmless.** `GraphPublisher` wrote `(collected[requirement.plugin] ??= {})[requirement.slot] = sourceValues`, so N derived bindings of one slot had one destination and the last edge in canonical order was the only one that arrived. That was survivable only because ADR-052 moved goal delivery off that channel: `SolveMember.goal` carries the qualified member id, the publisher joins it onto `SolverMember.goal`, and `readGoals` reads `member.goal`. Nothing read `inputs.ik["targets[seg-6]"]`, which means the uniqueness rationale in the old `solver-slots.ts` header was describing a channel no consumer used.

The two honest options were to correct that paragraph or to fix the channel. The channel is fixed: a `memberKey`-bearing binding is delivered as `collected[plugin][slot][memberKey]`, frozen where it is assembled, in `compareEdges` order. Relying on "nothing reads it" is how the next consumer of `collected[plugin][slot]` inherits a last-write-wins bug with no diagnostic. `ik` still reads its goals off `member.goal`, and that is not a duplicate channel: the join is the only thing that carries the qualified member id, which a plugin holding no graph cannot recover from an authored key.

**The shape rules generalize; exactly one stays keyed on the name.** The plan said the `keyframes-targets-*` rules stay keyed on the literal because they run with no registry. That is true of where they run and wrong about what they key on. Keyed on the literal, a second plugin's dict is refused as `keyframes-requires-source` one layer *before* its own declaration can be read, so `PluginRequirement.dict` is unreachable for every slot except `targets` and the parser's generalization buys nothing.

So the entry rules are keyed on the value's shape and renamed for it: `keyframes-requires-dict-empty`, `keyframes-requires-dict-key` and `keyframes-requires-dict-source`. A diagnostic has to name what the author wrote, and `keyframes-targets-member` firing at `spring.requires.tensions.seg-a` names something else. `keyframes-targets-shape` survives under its own name, because it asserts that *this* slot carries a dict, which no rule keyed on a value can answer.

The bracket refusal survives with a corrected rationale rather than a corrected rule. It existed because an authored key containing one could forge the derived `targets[<memberId>]` identity, and that identity is gone. What is left is that a dict key is an authored name, authored names reserve `:`, and this slice's accepted-behavior line is that no rig gains an authored spelling it did not have. Widening the legal key set is a separate decision with no rig asking for it.

**Alternatives rejected.** Keeping the grammar beside the field is the shape where both representations exist and the next reader picks the wrong one. Widening `claimsSlot` to answer a shape question would make the registry a validation engine for a type system it does not have, and would keep two owners of "declared". Detecting the capability purely by shape, with no declaration, is the issue's own Proposal A, and it cannot produce the refusal the issue's own test list asks for: `validateKeyframes` holds no registry so it cannot know which slots accept a dict, and `#resolveRequirements` holds a registry but would see only a slot name that is legitimately declared, so a dict under `fk`'s `root` would be accepted and then ignored. A predicate on `PluginRequirement` instead of a boolean would let a plugin answer with code for a question with two answers.

**Consequences.** No authored document changes. `targets` is spelled the same way, every existing rig derives the same chain, and no published value moves; `DV-7` asserts the derivation and `ik-multi-goal.test.ts` and `resolve-solvers.test.ts` pass with no edit, which is the observable-behavior claim asserted by absence of diff.

Three rule ids are renamed and one is added in each direction, so a consumer branching on `keyframes-targets-empty`, `keyframes-targets-member` or `keyframes-targets-source` has to move. `docs/AUTHORED-SCHEMA.md` and `docs/guide/errors-and-diagnostics.md` both explained the bracket refusal by the derived slot identity and are corrected.

`ResolvedRequirement` carries `memberKey`, because without it two entries of one slot are two identical rows in the report and a report that cannot tell two real dependencies apart quietly loses one.

A dict-accepting slot is now open to any plugin as a declaration rather than an edit, which is the open/closed claim and the reason the slice is worth making. `DV-1` demonstrates it under a name other than `targets`.

**Supersedes in part.** [ADR-052](./ADR-052-goal-addressing-by-member-id.md) recorded `claimsSlot` as "the slot-side counterpart to `claimsKey`" and recorded `targets[<memberId>]` as a derived slot identity with one owner in `contract/`. Both records are retired. The authored form ADR-052 chose is unchanged and is the reason this slice is possible at all: keying goals by member id rather than by position is what made the value a dict in the first place. Its `SL-` evidence series retires with the predicate and the grammar it pinned, so citations of `SL-1` through `SL-8` are historical.

**Evidence.** Cases `DV-1` through `DV-12` in `packages/core/test/unit/domain/dict-valued-requirements.test.ts`. Three mutation guards are recorded in that file's header rather than as cases of their own: drop `memberKey` from `requirementIdentity` and `DV-4` goes red, drop the `dict` check and `DV-2` goes red, gate `goalBindingsOf` on `memberKey` alone and `DV-8` goes red. The red run for the slice and its `ci-logs` archive path are anchored in `SESSION-STATUS.md`.
