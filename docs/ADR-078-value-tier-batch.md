# ADR-078: The value tier gets a batch, and `#invalidateOne` stops being its only ending

**Status:** Accepted, 2026-09-13

**Context.** [Issue #362](https://github.com/chahyasantoso/motion5/issues/362) scanned the runtime surface for convenience verbs and found that the interesting gap was not either of the two the issue opened with. It was the value tier. `setValues`, `overrideValues`, `setKeyframe`, `removeKeyframe` and `seek` each return the `PatchBatch` of their own single invalidate, each ends at `#invalidateOne`, which is single-node by construction, and every one of them is refused inside a recipe by name. So a caller editing `n` nodes per frame publishes `n` times, notifies every subscriber `n` times and moves the sequence `n` times. `apps/ik-playground/src/scroll-reach.ts` said so in its own `commit()`, in a comment that read "Each value write owns its invalidate; this is not a multi-track transaction", while looping `setValues` and `setKeyframe` over `RIGS` on every qualifying scroll update. The structural tier got a transaction in ADR-064; the value tier never did.

Phases 1a through 5 of that issue are what made this landable. Phases 1a and 1b bought the read budget back and gated it, phase 2 landed the enumeration readers, phase 3 and 4 subtracted three duplicate members and one wrong guide line, and phase 5 refused a membership batch on a measurement. This is the one phase with a capability in it, and it is the one the issue did not ask for.

**Decision.** A second tier, `ProjectHandle.values(recipe)`, spelled like the one that already works. It returns the single `PatchBatch` of one invalidate, and it hands the recipe a frozen `ValueTransaction` declared in `contract/value-transaction.ts` carrying `seek`, `setValues` and `overrideValues` node-addressed plus `track` and `tryTrack` answering the ordinary `TrackHandle`. Three further things are decided here rather than left to the implementation, because each of them moves an ownership that another record already named.

**One: `#invalidateOne` keeps its body, and the decision goes above it.** The plan for this phase named the DRY move as `#invalidateSeeds(ids)` with `#invalidateOne(id)` delegating to it. Measured against the tree rather than reasoned about, that is the expensive spelling. `#invalidateOne` is named as the value tier's publication owner by ADR-069's "Where the report goes", by ADR-070's single-responsibility list, by ADR-071's explicit decision that it and `#flush` stay separate, by two sections of `runtime/project-runtime.md`, by a comment in `motion-driver-edit.test.ts`, and by `LV-5` and `PK-17`, which read its declaration and assert that `this.#graph.invalidate([nodeId])` and `this.#diagnostics.recordAll(batch.diagnostics)` are inside it. Moving those two lines one member down turns two green cases red for no behavioural reason and makes four records name a member that answers a different question.

So the batch decision is a new member in front of it, `#publishValue(nodeId)`: with no batch open it delegates and nothing about the single-node path moves; with one open it asserts liveness, records the node as a seed, and answers the deferred batch. Its callers are both endings of `#completeWrite`, `#removeKeyframe`'s no-op, and `seek`, which stops copying `invalidate` plus `recordAll` inline and routes through the owner instead. One condition, read once, at one place, and adding the tier deleted a copy rather than adding one. `#invalidateOne` is still the only place a single-node value publication happens. It is no longer the only ending a value write can have, and that is the amendment this record makes to ADR-069, ADR-070 and ADR-071 rather than an edit to any of them.

The multi-seed publication owner is not invented either. `#flush(touched)` already invalidates a seed list in one call and records the batch's diagnostics, and `#apply` already uses it for exactly this, so the batch reuses that one. Its return widens from `void` to the batch it published, or nothing when it published none, which is what lets `values` report an empty recipe from the one owner of the empty-seed decision rather than restating it. Its three existing callers own no answer and ignore the return, and no test reads its declaration.

**Two: the reentrancy rung splits rather than moves.** Every verb that publishes, mounts or commits has to refuse while a batch is open, because a narrowed surface handed to a callback is not a fence and the recipe closes over the handle. The value verbs must not, because they are the point. Adding the condition to `#refuseReentrant` refuses the tier it exists for; moving the rung out of `#writeValues` up to its four entry points spreads one ordering across five sites, which is the shape issues #298 and #310 both closed.

So it is a ladder, which is the idiom `#liveEntry` and `#writableEntry` already are. `#refuseValueReentrant(verb)` asks the existing two conditions in the existing order, an open recipe then a boundary in flight. `#refuseReentrant(verb)` calls it and adds the third, `value-batch-immediate`. The four value verbs and `seek` ask the first rung; `mount`, `unmount`, `signal`, `invalidate`, `#setTrigger` and `#setStagger` keep asking the second and gain the new refusal with no call site edited. `#commit` gains the structural twin, `value-batch-structural`, at the one member every structural verb reaches rather than at the six that reach it, and it goes after that member's existing in-flight condition so every answer that exists today is unchanged and only the new case is added. `values` inside `values` is refused by the same rung with no second spelling.

**Three: `invalidate` is refused inside a batch rather than joined to it.** It is a bare publication with no bookkeeping of its own, so inside a batch that publishes once it is either a no-op or a second publication, and it is the verb a Motion driver reaches, which is precisely the reentrancy this rung exists to refuse. Joining it would also make the batch's seed list depend on which hook a host happened to wire.

**Alternatives rejected.**

- A flag on the existing verbs, or a variadic `setValues` taking a record of nodes. Rejected because it gives one member two publication contracts decided by an argument, which is the shape this project has now refused on `setMotion`, on `setTrack`, on the boolean that meant two things, and on the alias phase 3 deleted.
- Staged verbs returning nothing. Rejected because `track(nodeId)` answers a `TrackHandle` whose value members are typed `PatchBatch` across batched and unbatched use, and `TrackHandle` is one interface. "Nothing" needs a member with two return contracts, a nullable nobody checks, or a second narrowed handle interface, and all three are refused by name. The deferred batch is the answer that already exists in this tree for exactly this question.
- Reachability inside `edit()`. Rejected on the rule that a verb which applies immediately is refused by name inside a transaction rather than deferred into it: a settle step has no inverse, so admitting a fallible value write into a structural recipe moves its refusal to after the graph committed. Two tiers that do not nest, refused by name in both directions.
- Widening `SchemaTransaction` with the value verbs instead of adding a second surface. Rejected for the same reason phase 2 declined to widen it with the readers: it would give one recipe two failure contracts, and interface segregation is why each tier gets the narrowed surface it needs.
- Deduplicating seeds in `#valueSeeds`. Rejected because `GraphRuntime.flush` already deduplicates, and a second owner of that is a second answer.
- Answering the recipe's own return value, as `edit` does. Rejected because the batch is the answer to this call: a member that answered both would need the caller to ask for the publication separately, and then the tier has two mechanisms again.

**Consequences.** One public member and one exported type are added, `ProjectHandle.values` and `ValueTransaction`. Nothing is removed and no existing signature or refusal set moves. A disposed project reached from inside a recipe is reported rather than answered, with `ProjectRuntime is disposed.` in place of the batch, which is the string the value tier already answers, so the two tiers do not start answering one condition two ways. Publication runs inside `#boundary`, because subscriber notification is injected code and a listener that disposes must not release the graph out from under the notification it is inside. `apps/ik-playground/src/scroll-reach.ts` becomes one `values(...)` call and its comment about owning its own invalidate is deleted rather than reworded, which is the lie detector under this whole record: the consumer the scan found is the consumer that stops looping publications.

**Evidence.** `RA-162` through `RA-169` in `packages/core/test/unit/runtime/value-batch.test.ts`, pushed as their own commit before the source, so the red run is a real run.

- `RA-162` is the counter and the oracle in one rig, on the optimisation guardrail. Three nodes, one publisher flush and one sequence move; then the same three edits one at a time against a second runtime, asserting three flushes and three sequence moves and asserting the two runtimes published identical patches. A counter alone cannot see a stale composer, and an oracle alone is green against a tier that never batched.
- `RA-163` asserts the deferred batch names its node, carries no patch, carries one `value-batch-deferred` diagnostic, and answers while the sequence is still unmoved.
- `RA-164` refuses a batch inside a batch and a batch inside a recipe, and asserts the accepting direction in the same rig.
- `RA-165` refuses `invalidate`, `mount`, `unmount` and `signal` as `value-batch-immediate` and `addTrack` and `TrackHandle.remove` as `value-batch-structural`, asserts nothing the refused structural verbs staged was committed, and asserts each verb applies normally once the batch has returned.
- `RA-166` drives both endings of `#completeWrite`, the escalated one that stages a Track and re-seeks and the direct one every static write takes, because the escalated ending is the one with a settle collector in front of its publication.
- `RA-167` is the empty recipe and the read-only recipe: no invalidate at all, sequence unmoved, an empty batch as the answer.
- `RA-168` is a throwing recipe: the caller's throw unwrapped, nothing published, and the next batch ordinary rather than a refusal against a batch nothing closed.
- `RA-169` is a recipe that disposes: the disposal reported in place of the batch, and no publication.

One scope correction against the plan for this phase, recorded because a plan's prescription is measured before it is implemented. The plan named `setKeyframe` joining through both of its paths, including the recompile a new key takes, as one of the eight cases. `RA-166` instead drives both endings of `#completeWrite`, which is the mechanism `setKeyframe`, `removeKeyframe` and the recompile all join through, and it does so without authoring a plugin group whose registry-free resolution is a second subject. Nothing on that path is left unowned; what is not asserted here is the group-authoring precondition, which `RA-106` already owns.
