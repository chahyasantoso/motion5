# ADR-097: A rule id is a closed union

- Status: accepted, and implemented on the branch that carries this record. The enumeration exists with a coverage gate, the six generic constructors take `RuleId`, `Diagnostic.ruleId` is typed by it, `Diagnostic` is one flat interface, each rule's severity and its `ids` ownership are stated once in one record in `contract/rule.ts`, and the producers take their `ids` as the argument list that record derives; the record stays intent as to merge, on documentation rule 2 of [README.md](./README.md). The inventory paragraphs below were corrected in place more than once, and the corrections replace the claims rather than being appended beside them; so are the consequences and the follow-up, for that same reason rather than a weaker one. This record has not been accepted onto `main`. It lands with the branch it describes, every slice it records is on that branch, and the superseding convention in [PR-WORKFLOW.md](./PR-WORKFLOW.md) exists so that a reader cannot mistake a retired decision for a current one. A marker naming a sentence written and corrected before either was ever merged would say that one pull request superseded itself, which is the single self-contradicting file that convention's own second exception refuses. The totals they used to state are gone, because the gate is the authority a count kept pretending to be.
- Date: 2026-09-18
- Issue: [#449](https://github.com/chahyasantoso/motion5/issues/449), deferred from [#443](https://github.com/chahyasantoso/motion5/issues/443) section 2.5.

## Context

`Diagnostic.ruleId` is `string`, so the set of rule ids this project refuses by name is open. Nothing enumerates it, nothing can switch on it, and a reader asking which rules exist has to grep for a construction site. That is the open side ADR-092 describes one level out: a closed union read by predicates is closed on one side only, and here the union was never closed at all.

The inventory this record is built on was taken at `1cf86fbb3f9c5fb8b8c9619e8daef570f73f1a64` and it found more than the issue assumed. Three findings changed the decision.

Many more ids are assigned to `Diagnostic.ruleId` than the handful the issue sketches, and they are constructed across more modules than the twelve the first inventory named. No total is stated, here or anywhere else in this record. Three inventories of this union disagreed with each other, in both directions, so a number written down here would pin the error rather than catch it, and the coverage case in `packages/core/test/contract/rule-id.test.ts` is the authority instead; it asserts coverage and never a total, for the same reason. The modules named below are where a reader looks, not the proof. Fourteen construct a diagnostic directly: `packages/core/src/contract/validate-v5.ts`, `contract/migrate-v4-to-v5.ts`, `graph/ir.ts`, `graph/order.ts`, `graph/references.ts`, `domain/plugins.ts`, `domain/keyframe-compiler.ts` (now `contract/keyframe-compiler.ts`, moved by [ADR-105](./ADR-105-adapters-do-not-depend-on-domain.md)), `domain/track.ts`, `adapters/graph-builder/incremental.ts`, `adapters/trigger-factory/default.ts`, `runtime/graph-publisher.ts`, `runtime/graph-runtime.ts`, `runtime/project-runtime.ts`, and `runtime/report.ts`. The fifteenth, `runtime/publisher-outcome.ts`, mints ids on the error classes `graph-publisher.ts` forwards. `contract/diagnostics.ts` was named in that list and belongs out of it: it declares the constructor and no id. Seven base ids carry severity `warning` and the rest carry `error`; the prefixed spellings of `stop-missing-start` and `stop-missing-end` inherit it, so nine members of the union are warnings. Six and eight were the answer when the inventory was taken, and the seventh base warning is `reentrant-flush-deferred-frame`, minted by the follow-up below rather than missed by a count.

The named failures in `runtime/refusal.ts` are a second population, and they are not rule ids. `describeRefusal` renders `schema-transaction-nested`, `schema-transaction-immediate`, `value-batch-immediate`, `schema-commit-reentrant`, `value-batch-structural`, `keyframe-group-unbound` and `keyframe-goal-slot-reserved` as a prefix inside a thrown human message, and `project-runtime.ts` and `domain/plugins.ts` add `schema-transaction-recreated` and `plugin-input-collision` the same way. None of the nine is ever assigned to `Diagnostic.ruleId`, and the intersection of the two populations is empty. The issue names `keyframe-group-unbound` and `reserved-goal-slot` as rule ids carrying `ids`, which is the one factual error in it: those are refusal payload fields rendered into a sentence, and `refusal.ts` already holds them as a closed, minted, exhaustively read union. They need nothing from this record.

One construction site builds its id at run time, and it is the reason a literal scan cannot close this union by itself. `validate-v5.ts` pushes `issue(prefix + (aliases[ruleId] ?? ruleId), rulePath, message, severity)`, where the prefix and the alias map are data. That is what mints the whole `plugin-contribution-` family, including the four aliased stop rules. `graph-publisher.ts` picks between `observation-missing-upstream`, `observation-input-shape`, `observation-output-shape`, `composition-failure` and `composition-output-shape` through a branch on the error class; the fifth is forwarded from `CompositionOutputError.ruleId` and the first inventory missed it. `graph-runtime.ts` forwards one of `clock-tick-regression`, `clock-consumer-failure`, `flush-failure` and `scheduler-failure` through `#report`. Those are narrowable. The prefix one is not, unless the prefix becomes part of the type, and the prefix reaches the site through `validateKeyframes` options declared `ruleIdPrefix?: string` and `ruleIdAliases?: Readonly<Record<string, string>>`. Nothing constrains either, so this union is closed by closing those two options and not by typing the field alone.

The `ids` field is worse than optional. Some rules carry a fixed pair, `graph-cycle` carries a whole cycle path, `ik-target-not-single-leaf` carries a solver id followed by every leaf, and several plugin rules carry an arity that depends on which branch refused. A tuple per rule is not available for most of them.

## Decision

Rule ids become one closed union, `RuleId`, owned by a new `packages/core/src/contract/rule-id.ts`, and `Diagnostic.ruleId` is typed by it.

The prefixed family is stated as a template literal type over the keyframe rule family rather than enumerated twice, because the prefix is real and enumerating both halves would put one fact in two lists that a later slice keeps in step by hand. The type applies the alias substitution rather than unioning an independently maintained alias list beside it, so `plugin-contribution-stop` is derived from `stop-position` and `plugin-contribution-stop-position` is unrepresentable, and the map stays the one owner of the aliasing. The family covers every keyframe rule name the prefix can be applied to rather than the subset the adapter reaches today, and that over-approximation is deliberate: the reachable subset was measured wrong twice, and `plugin-contribution-keyframes-reserved-separator` is constructed in the tree while being absent from both reachable lists. A family covering every name the prefix can carry can never fail to type a real diagnostic, where a hand-picked reachable subset fails the first time a different rule fires.

`ids` ownership is stated per rule, in one record, and it is ownership rather than arity. `contract/rule.ts` answers two questions for every member of the enumeration: the severity the rule refuses at, and whether it names ids. Ownership has two answers and not three. `"sometimes"` was the third and it had exactly two causes, neither of which survived measurement. `observation-source` reported two different refusals under one id and is split: the shape refusal, for a source that is absent, empty or not a string and so has nothing to name, becomes `observation-source-shape` and owns none, while the qualification refusal keeps the id and owns its payload always. And `live-value-key`, `stale-motion-handle` and `stale-track-handle` are minted on thrown error classes with no construction site at all, which is an absent measurement rather than a third answer, so they own none and the first site that forwards one into a diagnostic with a payload fails at the point the decision is owed.

The enforcement is at the producer rather than in the shape of `Diagnostic`, and that is a correction to this record's own first draft. Three variants keyed by ownership were declared, and they were paid for on the public surface, because `Diagnostic` is exported from the package entry. They bought nothing on the read side: no production reader pattern-matches one, `runtime/patch-registry.ts` compares rule ids for identity and `runtime/refusal.ts` renders them generically. So `Diagnostic` is one interface, and what refuses a payload a rule does not own is `OwnedIds` at the call site, derived from that one record. A diagnostic carrying ids the rule does not own still fails `typecheck` rather than being ignored by a reader, and so does one omitting ids its rule always names; the mechanism is the argument list and not the variant, which is the part the first draft put in the wrong place.

`severity` is derived from the same record, through `ruleSeverity`, which is now the only expression in the tree that answers the question rather than the constructor's default in place of the literal `"error"` it used to be. That old default was the whole of the bug: a rule's own answer was a suggestion, a caller could overrule it by passing something else, and a caller overrode it silently by passing nothing, which made every warning rule reported by such a caller an error diagnostic that nothing noticed. Passing nothing is what almost every caller does, so correcting the default is what closed it.

The parameter is deleted, and both of the corrections this paragraph has carried are recorded here in place rather than beside. It first claimed the invariant while only the default had moved, which is the overclaim this record's own status paragraph exists to catch; it then said the parameter survives, which was true when it was written and is not true now. Nineteen sites named a severity, every one named the severity its rule already fixes, so no diagnostic moved when the parameter went, and the compiler enumerating those sites is what the deletion was owed rather than a fourth hand count. `contract/validate-v5.ts` forwards a rule id, a path and a message and its own local helper carries no `= "error"` default any more, and `adapters/trigger-factory/default.ts` names no severity. There is no argument position left in which a call site could name one, so this is the invariant a reader might have taken it for, stated only now that it is true.

The fixed pair this record used to promise is withdrawn, and the measurement is why. Arity is not checkable where a diagnostic is built: every `ids` in this tree arrives through `Object.freeze([...])`, which widens a two-element literal to `readonly string[]` before any type sees it, so a `readonly [string, string]` field would refuse the code that already constructs it correctly. Arity is not single-valued either. `observation-source` names one id at one site and none at another, `plugin-unknown-key` names one at three sites and two at a fourth, and `plugin-contribution-shape` names one at one site and two at another. A group per arity would have had to declare the wrong one for three rules to keep the build green, which is the per-rule lie one level down. Ownership is the part of the claim that is both true and enforced, so it is the only part the type states; the arities are in the inventory the coverage case measures against, not in a declaration.

The generic constructors are narrowed at the same time, and there are six rather than five. `contract/diagnostics.ts` `diagnostic`, `graph/ir.ts` `diag`, `runtime/report.ts` `frozenDiagnostic`, `domain/keyframe-compiler.ts` `diagnostic`, the plugin `diagnostic`, and `graph-runtime.ts` `#report` all took `string` when this was written, and a closed union whose only producer accepts `string` is closed nowhere. All six take `RuleId` now, and the five that are not the constructor forward to it rather than building a frozen shape of their own, so the shape has one owner and the severity has one answer. `frozenDiagnostic` was attributed to `contract/diagnostics.ts` by the first inventory and it lives in `runtime/report.ts`, which declares `diagnostic` and not `frozenDiagnostic`. Narrowing them is the slice, not a follow-up to it.

The ten refusal prefixes stay where they are. They are already a closed union in `refusal.ts`, read by two total switches, and pulling them into `RuleId` would give one namespace two owners and would make `describeRefusal` depend on the contract layer for a string it templates itself.

The enumeration gets a gate, on the rule the evidence-id gate earned in issue #283: where a gate reads a hand-maintained list of subjects, a case asserts that the list covers the subjects actually present. A scan that only reads literals would answer absent for the entire prefixed family, so the case asserts it found the prefix site too. It asserts coverage and never a total: this inventory has been wrong twice, so a number in the gate would pin the error rather than catch it.

## Alternatives considered

A branded nominal type over `string`. Rejected: it closes assignment and not enumeration, so nothing can switch on it and the acceptance criterion asking for one type listing every id is unmet.

Enumerating the prefixed family as 22 more literals beside the base ones. Rejected on the DRY rule this project already applies to derived facts: the prefix is derived from the base name at run time, so deriving it in the type too is one owner, and two hand-maintained lists is the shape issue #283 refused.

A tuple per rule for `ids`, as the issue sketches. Rejected as unavailable: `graph-cycle` and `ik-target-not-single-leaf` have no fixed arity, and pinning a false one would either refuse a real diagnostic or force a cast at the site that builds it.

Splitting `Diagnostic` into one interface per severity. Rejected: severity is not the question any reader asks, and ADR-016 keeps runtime diagnostics inline on one channel.

## Consequences

Adding a rule breaks the build at `rule-id.ts`, which is where the id is named, rather than being inherited silently by every consumer that pattern-matches a string. That is the acceptance criterion the issue asks for.

This touches `contract/v5`, and `Diagnostic` is exported from the package entry, so a consumer that constructs a `Diagnostic` with its own rule id stops compiling. That is a public surface change and it is owed a version decision in the slice that lands it, not here.

The alias map in `validate-v5.ts` stops being free data and becomes a typed mapping. That is the cost of the prefix being in the type, and it is the only place it is paid.

Ownership of `ids` is enforced at the producer, and the boundary that remains is worth stating rather than implying. A diagnostic is checked at the call site that builds it, against the argument list its rule owns. There is no variant left to be checked against and no narrowing for an assertion to reach: with `Diagnostic` flat, `asDiagnostic` was a widening rather than a correlation stated once, so the "boundary stated rather than implied" this paragraph was written to describe stopped describing a union, and the widening was deleted with the last of the raw producers it survived for. `DiagnosticFields` went with it. That closes the hole this paragraph used to bound rather than merely bounding it: nothing can name a rule by a literal and hand over a payload it does not own, because no expression accepts the two independently any more. This is therefore an invariant over every construction rather than an assertion boundary, and it is stated only now that every construction goes through one call. Each producer takes its `ids` as `OwnedIds` of the rule id it was handed, so a call naming a rule that owns no ids cannot pass one and a call naming a rule that always names ids cannot omit them. The derivation is guarded by a one-element tuple on each side of `extends`, because a naked conditional distributes over `RuleId` and would answer with the union of both lists, which is every existing caller broken rather than one boundary left open. With ownership down to two answers there is no third list to fall through to, so the optional case is reached only where the compiler cannot prove that every member of `Rule` shares one answer: an open `RuleId`, or a union spanning both answers, for which neither guarded `extends` succeeds. That second case is a limit rather than a bug, and it is stated here because the deleted record stated it and a reader would not suspect it: this correlates one rule id and not a set of them. No call site in this tree holds such a union. Two producers forward a rule id they hold as data rather than as a literal, and neither is an open question any more. `contract/validate-v5.ts` reaches the constructor through `scopedRuleId`, whose return type is narrowed to `KeyframeRuleId | ContributionRuleId`; every member of both families owns no ids, so the derived list is the empty one and the forwarding call is checked against it rather than accepted by a branch with no claim in it. `runtime/report.ts` reaches it through the private `frozenDiagnostic`, which is reached only from the exported member that derives its own list. The first is backed by the compiler and the second by the record in `contract/rule.ts`.

## Follow-up

The work was cut into five slices so no one of them exceeded the sizing rule in [PR-WORKFLOW.md](./PR-WORKFLOW.md), and all five have landed on the branch that carries this record. First the enumeration and its coverage gate, with `Diagnostic.ruleId` still `string`, so the list was proved complete before anything depended on it. Then the six generic constructors narrowed to `RuleId`, and the prefix and alias options became one `scope`. Then `Diagnostic.ruleId` was typed, which is where the last open producer surfaced: `publishFailureRule` returned `string`, so the union was closed at the field and open at the function computing the field, and the publisher forwarding its answer into a `Diagnostic` is what said so. Then `ids` was discriminated by ownership. Then the five producers took their `ids` as the list `OwnedIds` derives, which this record used to name as a slice of its own and price at every call site of five constructors. It cost five signatures and no call site, and the reason is the rule about a prescription being measured before it is implemented: the ownership record was read off those very call sites, so every one of them already passed the arity its rule owns. A census over `graph/ir.ts`, `contract/validate-v5.ts`, `domain/plugins.ts`, `domain/keyframe-compiler.ts`, `runtime/report.ts` and the suite found 27 `diag` calls, 26 direct constructor calls behind 24 helper invocations, 21 plugin calls, 7 test calls and none to correct. The price this record predicted was the price of writing the change without measuring it first.

A sixth slice followed those five on the same branch, and it is the one that replaced the three variants with one record. `contract/rule.ts` states each rule's severity and its `ids` ownership, `Diagnostic` became one interface, `IdsOwnership` lost its third answer, `observation-source` split into a shape refusal and a qualification refusal, `graph/ir.ts`'s `diag`, which held a hardcoded `severity: "error"` and a conditional `ids` spread, was collapsed into the one constructor, the equivalent producers in `runtime/report.ts`, `domain/plugins.ts` and `domain/keyframe-compiler.ts` were collapsed after it, the four raw object literals in `graph/order.ts`, `graph/references.ts` and `runtime/graph-publisher.ts` were the last of the second ownership and forward through the constructor now, the `severity` parameter itself was deleted, and `contract/diagnostic-ids.ts` was deleted. One further correctness question fell out of the ownership record rather than out of the rename: both deferred rules in `runtime/report.ts` answer `ids: "always"`, and `OwnedIds` proves that an argument was passed and never that it says anything, so a deferral was able to publish a warning whose payload named nothing. `DeferredSeeds` requires a seed list with at least one member, which makes that unrepresentable rather than merely unreached. Making it unrepresentable moved the question rather than closing it, which a quality pass over that slice found and which is recorded here because the fix is this record's mechanism rather than that one's. A reentrant call whose deferral names no seed still has to be answered, and it was answered with the empty batch, which is a claim that nothing was queued. That claim is true of a request that deferred neither a seed nor a frame and false of one that deferred a frame, so the two are told apart by what the payload owes rather than by what the request stated, and the second answers under `reentrant-flush-deferred-frame`, a rule owning no ids because a frame is not a node. This is the `observation-source` split a second time, for the same cause and with the same prefix-preserving spelling: one id was carrying two refusals, one of which has nothing to name. The same read decides the booking, so a deferral owing nothing no longer books a drain whose only act is to release its own booking. ADR-088's decision two is unchanged by either half and is what both restore. What it bought over the fourth slice is that a rule answers once: two records answered four rules differently while both existed, so the deletion is what makes the answer single rather than merely primary. It landed in steps, each with its own exact tested SHA on the owning pull request, and the order was forced rather than chosen: while both maps existed a producer had to read `OwnedIds` from whichever module the constructor it forwards to reads, and mixing them was a compile error, so every consumer moved in one request.

The fourth slice also promised that every consumer pattern-matching a rule id would become a total switch, and that half is withdrawn rather than implemented, because it has no subjects. No production reader pattern-matches a rule id at all: `runtime/patch-registry.ts` compares ids for identity and `runtime/refusal.ts` renders them generically. The exhaustive read this decision actually owes is the record in `contract/rule.ts`, keyed by the enumeration, which is as closed as a `switch` ending at `unreachable` and needs no reader to be, on the same argument `SCOPED_RULE_ID` makes one level up. That read was `contract/diagnostic-ids.ts` keyed by `BaseRuleId`, and it is deleted: it held a second answer for four rules, and a producer reading one map while the constructor read the other was a compile error rather than a drift that went unnoticed, which is what made replacing it atomic rather than incremental. The owning pull request carries the exact tested SHA.

Two of the four names the first inventory called documentation-only were misread, and the enumeration is where that became visible. `trigger-driver-unavailable` has a constructor in `adapters/trigger-factory/default.ts` and ADR-030 documents it as a load-time diagnostic, so it is an ordinary member of the union rather than drift, and finding it is what proved the coverage scan has to walk `adapters/`. `keyframe-require-shape` is rendered inside a thrown `TypeError` in `domain/authoring/keyframes.ts` and is never assigned to `Diagnostic.ruleId`, so it belongs to the message-prefix population rather than to this union. Only `ik-solver-unsupported-arity` and `ik-solved-pivot-unsupported` are prose about nothing, and the documents naming them are corrected by the slice that has a reader for the claim rather than by this record.
