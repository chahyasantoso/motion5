- **Per-member solved-rotation weight ([issue #211](https://github.com/chahyasantoso/motion5/issues/211))** is **complete** on `feat/ik-member-weight-blend` in [PR #219](https://github.com/chahyasantoso/motion5/pull/219), with ADR-055 and cases `WT-1` through `WT-16`. `fk.values.weight` blends a bone's authored local `rotation` toward the angle its solver published, along the shorter of the two arcs between them, per member rather than per solver so that a chain can stagger its reach.
- The default is `1`, and that is a hard requirement rather than a goal. It is exactly the unconditional override the key replaces, so every solver-bound rig authored before it composes byte-identically and not one existing `IK-`, `FB-`, `PV-`, `FO-` or `RS-` case moved. `WT-5` is the instrument that says so: it composes a bone whose authored `rotation` _is_ the solved angle, with no solver bound, as its oracle, so it passes against the reverted blend too. `WT-2` pins both endpoints as byte identities returned by short-circuit rather than by arithmetic, because the wrap sends a separation past half a turn to its complement and would move every published rotation more than 180 degrees from its authored one by a whole turn.

Every existing `ik` test staying green with zero edits is the proof the clamp moved rather than being rewritten.

- `Track` composition has two halves that can be reached separately, and reaching them separately changes nothing. `interpolated()` strips the interpolator's underscore scratch and returns the renderer-neutral state; `composeFrom(seed, inputs)` runs the plugin chain from `seed`; `compose(inputs)` delegates to both in one line. `CF-1` is the equivalence, at three progress values, value for value.

- ADR-042's single filter point is unmoved. `publishableValues` still runs once per composition, after the chain and before the freeze, so a namespaced key is dropped there whether a plugin derived it or a seed carried it in. `CF-6` pins both directions.

## Accepted behavior, not remaining defects

- `ProjectHandle.seek(nodeId, progress)` is leaf-level scrubbing. On a driver-backed Motion, the next driver emission overwrites a seeked value.
- Owner-based `adopt` and `destroyAdopted` remain available for compatibility; current consumer code should use `addTrack` and `TrackHandle`.
- A scheduled job runs in its scheduler pass even when another job throws; the configured error boundary reports the failure once.
- A tick whose clock consumer fails still flushes the graph. The failure is attributed to the consumer boundary, not to graph flushing.
- A Motion is destroyed empty. Remove its tracks before destroying it.
- `fkPlugin` replaces local authored `rotation` with world-space `rotation` in its composed output. The local value is not published beside the world value.
- A flat authored `x` or `y` is `plugin-ambiguous-key` in any registry holding both `transform` and `fk`, exactly as a flat `rotation` already was. That is the stated cost of the pivot offset rather than a regression, `FO-6` asserts it, and the two W2 adoption fixtures that authored a flat `x` incidentally are migrated into `transform.values`.
- A bone that authors no offset composes the frame it always composed, because both keys default to zero rather than being absent. `FO-1` is that guard and passes on the parent by design.
- A bone that authors no `weight` composes what the unconditional override composed, to the byte, because the key defaults to `1` rather than being absent. `WT-5` is that guard and passes against the reverted blend by design.
- A `weight` on a bone that bound no solver anywhere is inert and is not refused, and that is a boundary rather than a gap. `weight` is claimed by `fkPlugin` and ADR-043 lets any other plugin claim it, and `resolveSolvers` holds no registry, so with no solve in reach it cannot tell a blend weight from another plugin's own live input: `Q-10`'s `reach` plugin is exactly that shape and predates the key. `WT-14` states it, and `WT-10` pins the composition that makes it harmless. Same boundary that keeps a member's flat `rotation` out of `ik-solved-rotation-dead`.
- A blend at weight `0` and a bone with no solver bound are two different things, deliberately. The `solved === undefined` short-circuit is what keeps them apart, which is why the weight is composed around `readSolvedRotation` rather than folded into it: folding it in would make an unbound slot and a bone that currently ignores its solve the same answer.
- The tie at exactly half a turn blends in the positive direction. Both arcs are equally short there, so the choice is arbitrary and is pinned by `WT-4` rather than left to the arithmetic: a rewrite reaching for `floor` instead of `ceil` would silently flip a pose with every other case still green.
- A solver node publishes its own authored values beside `rotations`, so `flip` is readable off its patch. That is the chaining rule rather than a convenience: `Track.composeFrom` replaces, so a composer returning only its output deletes its own track's keys.
- The default elbow is the positive branch, `phi + alpha`. Both branches put the tip exactly on the target, so the convention is pinned by two numbers (`40.168` and `-51.318` for the worked rig) rather than by a reachability assertion either branch satisfies.
- A composite output never reaches a renderer. `renderableValues` skips a value that is still a plain record after serialization, which is deliberately after rather than before: a plugin that serializes a composite into something renderable still renders.
- A solver that reaches `GraphPublisher` with no `root` edge throws rather than defaulting to a plugin name. It is unreachable through `resolveSolvers`, and `ik-solver-no-root` is what keeps it that way; a publisher that guesses a scope is the thing the lookup exists to delete.
- A member's flat authored `rotation` is not `ik-solved-rotation-dead`, and that is a boundary rather than a gap. Which plugin owns a flat key is the registry's question and `resolveSolvers` holds no registry by design, so a member authoring one meets `plugin-ambiguous-key` in any registry with two claimants and `plugin-unknown-key` in one with none. `RS-9` states it.
- A rig that breaks a reference and a chain rule at once reports the reference error alone. The chain rule is derived from the edges the reference error says are wrong, so reporting both would name a member for a typo one node up. Fix the source and the next build reports whatever the chain still gets wrong.
- `Track.interpolated()` is a read. It runs no plugin, memoizes nothing, and does not mark the track clean, so asking a track what it interpolated cannot make it look composed. It also reports no `base`: where a member sits in its chain is topology, and `SolveMember.base` already answers for it.
- `compose` now builds its seed on every call, including the calls the memo answers. Reading `#timeline.state` and comparing the result is the price of one memo owner instead of two, and it is paid only where the old dirty flag would have returned early: on a dirty track the flag still short-circuits the comparison entirely. Two caches with two invalidation rules would have been cheaper per tick and wrong the first time one of them drifted.
- `composeFrom` retains copies of both memo keys rather than the caller's own objects, so a seed mutated after the fact cannot turn a stale snapshot into a memo hit.
- A refused Track replacement leaves the previous compiled Track live and retryable; a successful replacement preserves the Motion entry index and stagger timing.
- An authored observation `target`, `role`, or `projection` is an error rather than an ignored key, for the same reason `use` is. Both packages are unpublished at `0.0.0`, so no removal carries a compatibility shim.
- A generic `observes` entry cannot feed a track's own composition, by design. There is no authored form for an input edge; a plugin requirement is the only one.
- An input edge carrying no requirement is unreachable by construction and throws `observation-input-shape` naming the edge.
- The core test suite reaches the fakes by relative source path, not through `@motion5/core/testing`.
- No author may animate a flat property named `values`, and no plugin may claim that key at group level. Both are the existing `requires` precedent, and the reservation is on section position: a leaf named `values` inside the section is an ordinary property, which `Y-8` pins.
- A group may author `requires` with no `values`. That is not an omission to be refused; it is how a plugin joins the composer chain to receive an upstream value without animating anything of its own. `Y-11` pins it, and `remount`, `cross-motion`, and `phase4-dynamic-lifecycle` author exactly that shape and needed no migration at all.
- `{ fk: {} }` is an accepted no-op property rather than a group, because it names no section. `Y-6` guards it, since it is easy to break by accident.
- An empty array is an accepted leaf that compiles to nothing, exactly as `{ stops: [] }` was. It is not an error, because a sequence with no stops has no position to report on.
- `stops-shape` keeps its rule id after ADR-050 even though its message changed. The animated form still is stops, and renaming it would churn its `plugin-contribution-stops-shape` alias and every citation to express a distinction that does not exist.
- A static leaf on a prepare-stage contributor's key is refused rather than handed an empty stop list. No static leaf existed before ADR-050, so the blast radius today is zero; a real prepare-stage use for one is a designed extension with its own issue.
- An object is never a static value, and it is refused as `keyframes-missing-values-section` rather than as `stops-shape`. `{ x: { hold: 1 } }` and `{ fk: { length: 62 } }` are the same shape, a key holding a record of one scalar, so no predicate on the value can report the first as a bad leaf and the second as a group missing its section. ADR-049 introduced `looksLikeLegacyGroup` precisely to stop that shape being misdiagnosed as a property of an unknown shape, and `LF-12` pins it with a scalar leaf on purpose. `LF-10` therefore asserts the invariant it actually owns, that no object is a legal static value, and lets the more specific rule id say it.
- `LF-16` reads code with its comments and quoted bodies stripped. Its probe is a regex, and the three modules that own the refusal all have to name the retired form: `contract/authored-leaf.ts` and `contract/v5.ts` in a doc comment, and `contract/validate-v5.ts` inside the diagnostic message an author reads. Scanning that prose left the gate unsatisfiable by exactly the code that satisfies it, and the alternative was to reword a diagnostic to dodge a grep, which would make product text a function of an assertion's imprecision. It is the same reasoning that already puts `docs/` out of scope, applied one level down: an authored leaf is object syntax and never lives inside a sentence.

## Guardrails

- One owner per state transition and one owner per normalization.
- No compatibility flags, facades, placeholder tests, renderer imports, or core GSAP imports.
- Every behavioral recovery slice starts with failing-first evidence and archives the failed run when the pipeline supports it.
- Failing-first means failing assertions. A red run that failed `typecheck` or `format:check` ran no test at all, so it is a broken test file rather than evidence, and the fix is a test commit followed by a fresh red run.
- Failing-first for a seam that does not exist yet declares that seam in the test file and deletes the declaration when the source lands. A cast that makes a red run reach its assertions is scaffolding with a stated expiry, not a type hole to keep.
- A green case is only evidence of what it would fail without. A test whose assertions survive the fix being reverted, the two branches of a flag being swapped, or its twenty inputs collapsing to one input is a green test with no subject, and it is rewritten rather than counted.
- Docs, public types, tests, and status move together.
- A cited evidence case id names exactly one test in the suite.
- Formatting is a hard CI gate; hand-padded Markdown tables are avoided.
- A key may have several claimants; the authored group names the owner. A plugin-owned requirement is scoped and never merged into authored values.
- A field with no consumer is removed and then refused, never left declared or silently ignored.
- One question, one mechanism. Where two mechanisms answer "where does an upstream value land" and one of them can overwrite an authored value, the weaker one is deleted rather than documented as discouraged.
- A declared entrypoint carries a tier, and a tier a consumer may not import is enforced by a gate that can see every declared workspace. Prose alone is not enforcement.
- An authored section is reserved by name rather than inferred from shape. Where a spelling would otherwise mean two things depending on context, the name is taken out of the author's namespace and the ambiguity stops existing instead of being resolved.
- A wrapper that exists only to hold one value is deleted, and the thing it held becomes the value. Where the wrapper let an author express something that should not be expressible, the replacement makes it unrepresentable by shape rather than rejected by a rule.
- A value that never changes does not pay the cost of one that does. Where a syntax change would only rename that cost, the cost is removed instead, or the change is not worth making.
- A gate reads code, never prose. Where a scan for a retired form would flag the module that documents or refuses it, the scan is narrowed to what the form can actually be written in; the product text is not reworded to satisfy the scan.
- Two assertions that demand different rule ids for one shape are a design question, not a bug to route around. The more specific rule wins and the weaker assertion is rewritten to state the invariant it actually owns.
- A helper that already exists and already does the arithmetic is called, not copied. Where a module exports a primitive its own composer reinlines, the reinlined copy is the deletion the slice is for.
- A cache is keyed on everything its answer depends on. Where a flag reports a different question than the one being asked, adding a second cache is not the fix; the key is widened and the one owner keeps it. Where widening it would reproduce another owner's key, it is deleted.
- A misconfiguration with no symptom is refused by name at load time. Where a derivation can only skip a shape it cannot answer for, the skip is a diagnostic, because a rig that holds a broken pose silently is worse than one that fails to load.
- A rule answers for the scope that owns the thing it refuses. Where a check reads wider than the owner it belongs to, it reports for plugins it knows nothing about, and the fix is the narrower read rather than a documented exception. A rule that reserves an authored key across the whole registry is the same defect wearing the opposite shape.
- A derivation runs over input an earlier gate accepted. Where it reads what a failed validation already refused, it answers about something that never existed, so it runs after the bail rather than beside it and the first thing an author reads is the cause.
- A refactor that only exists to unblock a later slice still ships alone, with its own equivalence evidence. Reviewed inside the feature, a pure refactor is read as part of the design it was extracted from.
- A default that exists to keep every prior rig byte-identical is proved by the prior rigs, not by a new test. Where not one existing worked number moves, that is the evidence; where one has to be edited, the default is broken.
