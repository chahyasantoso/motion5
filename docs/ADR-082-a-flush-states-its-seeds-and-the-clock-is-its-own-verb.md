# ADR-082: A flush states its seeds, and the clock transition is its own verb

**Status:** Accepted, 2026-09-16

**Context.** [Issue #374](https://github.com/chahyasantoso/motion5/issues/374) reads `GraphRuntime` at `746f1bb05ddd1e2343e5e34680f50abb3d0fe7ef`, which is main after [#372](https://github.com/chahyasantoso/motion5/pull/372) and [#373](https://github.com/chahyasantoso/motion5/pull/373), and finds one member carrying two operations. `flush(seeds: readonly string[], tick?: number)` publishes for a seed list, and additionally validates finiteness and monotonicity and moves `#lastTick` when a second argument is present. Which of the two a caller performed was stated by argument count, which is the weakest statement available, and the half that was reachable by omission was the half that consumes a frame number the clock will reuse on its next frame. `invalidate(seeds)` was `return this.flush(seeds);` after #373 deleted its duplicate liveness assertion, so the class also carried two names for the no-tick operation. Both halves of that are the same defect, counted once per axis.

**Decision one. `flush(seeds)` loses the tick, and `flushAtTick(seeds, tick)` gains it as a required parameter.** This is the issue's proposal, accepted as written. A caller of `flush` cannot reach the clock's frame number, and a caller of `flushAtTick` cannot avoid stating the transition. The segregation stops being a convention a reader has to hold and becomes a signature: `#flushTick` is the only caller of `flushAtTick` in `src`, so a live edit remains structurally unable to consume a frame number the clock will reuse, which is the property `clock-tick-identity` reads from the other side and which #373 protected by keeping a second member instead.

**Decision two. `invalidate` is deleted, and ADR-081 loses its premise rather than its reasoning.** ADR-081's decision two refused this deletion, and the argument was not sentiment: `invalidate` was `flush` with the clock's parameter removed, so deleting it and letting `ProjectRuntime.#invalidateSeeds` call `flush` directly would have **widened** what the project tier could reach. That argument was correct at that head and decision one spends it. After decision one, `flush(seeds)` is exactly as narrow as `invalidate(seeds)` was, so keeping both is a second name for one operation, which is the defect ADR-081 objects to one axis over. A reader arriving at ADR-081's decision two should read it as true of the tree it measured and superseded here; nothing about its decision one moves, and `seeds` is still required.

**What the blast radius says, measured rather than feared.** ADR-081 also priced the deletion, and half of that pricing is retired by decision one too. It says a spy repointed from `invalidate` to `flush` weakens the claim it makes, "because `flush` is also the tick path and the drain". After decision one `flush` is not the tick path, `flushAtTick` is, so a spy on `flush` observes strictly less than the same spy did before: the twenty `vi.spyOn(..., "invalidate")` spies that move to `"flush"` are each at least as strong as they were, and the local variable they bind stays named `invalidate` because the tier's operation was not renamed. The drain half of that worry survives and is why `#scheduleDrain` is left where it is, below.

**Shape, and the ordering that does not move.** The issue's sketch validates and advances the tick before the shared machinery runs. Implemented literally that is a behaviour change and a defect: today the reentrancy answer comes before the tick is consumed, so a flush requested from inside subscriber notification is deferred without moving `#lastTick`, which `publisher-reentrancy` asserts. So the ordering is preserved, and it is preserved by giving each step an owner rather than by placing a conditional carefully. `#deferIfFlushing(seeds)` owns the reentrancy answer for both public verbs and hands back the deferred batch rather than a boolean, because two callers that had to ask and then act could act differently. `#advanceTick(tick)` owns tick validity and the one write to `#lastTick`, and refuses before the field moves, so a rejected tick leaves the runtime on the frame it was already on. `#flushSeeds(seeds)` owns publication mechanics and nothing else, and its one precondition -- not currently flushing -- is stated rather than re-asked, because a second copy of a decided question is a second owner of it. Four owners, no optional parameter at any layer, and the ordering is now written in the two verbs instead of implied by where an `if` sits.

**Why `#flushSeeds` does not take an optional tick.** It would recreate exactly the shape this record is about, one layer down and out of reach of the type system, and the issue says so: the clock transition may not be accepted by the low-level flush operation. The two-line ordering statement each public verb carries is the price of that, and it is a price worth paying because those two lines are the difference between the operations rather than a duplicate of either.

**Why `#scheduleDrain` still spells `flush([])`.** Pointing it at `#flushSeeds` directly is tempting, because it would make a spy on `flush` mean "one caller-stated publication" exactly. It is refused: the drain is scheduled work, nothing guarantees a scheduler cannot run it while a flush is in flight, and the deferral fallback that protects it lives in the public verb. `flush([])` there still means "publish whatever `#pendingSeeds` carried" rather than "publish nothing", which is why an empty caller list remains a project-tier question owned by `ProjectRuntime.#publishSeeds` under ADR-080 and not a short-circuit here.

**Consequences.** No public surface and no behaviour. `GraphRuntime` is not exported from `packages/core/src/index.ts` and reaches no consumer in `apps/` or `packages/react`; no exported type, member, refusal, diagnostic or rule id moves; and every publication in `src` happens for the same seeds at the same sequence as before. `ProjectRuntime` changes one line, `#invalidateSeeds` reaching `this.#graph.flush(nodeIds)`, and keeps every name it had: `#publishSeeds` still owns what an empty seed list means, `#invalidateSeeds` is still the only place a seed list becomes a published recorded batch, `#invalidateOne` still owns the single-node report, and the public `ProjectRuntime.invalidate` verb keeps its name, signature and answer. `LV-5` moves three source-region literals and keeps its count at two, which is the point of the count: one occurrence, one owner. Thirty-three call sites move, all in tests, and that count is measured on the final head rather than estimated before it: twenty `vi.spyOn(..., "invalidate")` retargets across five files, and thirteen direct `GraphRuntime` calls across three. `membership-gating`'s seed-contract case is rewritten wholesale rather than moved, so it is counted apart from those thirty-three rather than folded into them. The thirty-one this record first carried undercounted the spies by three, because a bounded text search returns a limited number of matches per file and both `motion-consistency` and `demo-runtime-authoring` hold more than it returned. `tsc` named all three exactly, which is the useful half of that mistake: after decision one a spy on a member `GraphRuntime` no longer declares is a compile error rather than a silent runtime throw, so the type system is the exhaustive oracle for an inventory of this shape and a search is not.

**Where the reasoning lives.** `graph-runtime.ts` is 19,528 bytes at the final head, under the 30,000 that would oblige a sister document, so both public verbs and all three privates carry their reasoning in source docblocks and the citations there stay legal. ADR-059, ADR-069, ADR-070, ADR-078, ADR-080 and ADR-081 quote the old spelling and are deliberately not edited: this project supersedes a record with a record rather than amending a decided one, exactly as ADR-080 retired ADR-079's premise without touching it.

**Alternatives refused.** Keeping `invalidate` as an alias of `flush`, refused as decision two: after decision one it carries nothing `flush` does not, and twenty spies pointing at an alias would document a name rather than an operation. Implementing the issue's sketch literally, refused above as an ordering change that moves `#lastTick` for a flush that published nothing. Giving `#flushSeeds` an optional tick, refused above. Making one of the two verbs private so the other is the sole public entry, refused for the reason ADR-081 already recorded: `scheduler-reentrancy` drives the tick seam directly and the project tier drives the no-tick one, so both are public entries with real callers. Refactoring this class's lifecycle into a discriminated-union state machine, which is the issue's closing paragraph and is a real improvement rather than a fashion: `#disposed`, `#flushing` and `#scheduledDrain` are three independent booleans encoding one lifecycle with illegal combinations nothing prevents, and the snapshot memo is a second such state spread over three fields. It is deferred to its own slice rather than declined, and [issue #376](https://github.com/chahyasantoso/motion5/issues/376) carries the sketch above with its measured blast radius and its acceptance, because it is a second invariant with a second blast radius across `pendingSeeds`, `tick`, `sequence`, `lastFlushError`, `#report` and `publisher-snapshot-memo`, and landing it on top of thirty-three moving call sites would make one review that cannot separate a rename from a rewrite. `PR-WORKFLOW.md` asks for one meaningful invariant per slice, and this record honours that rather than arguing with it.
