# ADR-087: Publication evidence reads behaviour, and a scan says it is a scan

**Status:** Accepted, 2026-09-16

**Context.** The last three findings from the quality pass over [#375](https://github.com/chahyasantoso/motion5/pull/375), read at `1af7580557b0ad65a44c4216a0fde7170f33e203`. All three are the same defect in three shapes: evidence that describes the tree rather than the behaviour, and therefore goes stale without going red. [Issue #379](https://github.com/chahyasantoso/motion5/issues/379) proves a behavioural ownership claim with a whole-file string count. [Issue #381](https://github.com/chahyasantoso/motion5/issues/381) is twenty spies whose name and whose assertion both claim less than they check. [Issue #385](https://github.com/chahyasantoso/motion5/issues/385) is a paragraph explaining a case through a member that has not existed for two records. None of the three was a failing test, and that is the point: each one was green while being wrong.

**Decision one. The publication-ownership claim is split from the mechanism that measures it.** `LV-5` asserted "one publication owner and one diagnostics channel" by reading `project-runtime.ts` as text: an absence inside `#writeValues`, a presence inside `#invalidateSeeds`, and `source.split("this.#graph.flush(")` having length two. The claim is behavioural and the evidence was lexical, which was already true before #375 and which #375 made weaker in the two ways the issue names. The literal stopped being rare, because `this.#graph.invalidate(` was a member one caller in `src` ever spelled and `this.#graph.flush(` is the graph tier's ordinary verb. And it never could see the thing it guards against, since a second owner reaching the same member through a local alias, a bound function, a differently formatted call or a wrapper leaves the count where it was.

So `LV-5` keeps the runtime assertions and gains the behavioural half of the diagnostics claim: a publication carrying a diagnostic is recorded exactly once, injected through the spy and read back through `ProjectRuntime.diagnostics`. The mechanical scan moves whole to `packages/core/test/unit/runtime/publication-ownership.test.ts`, which is named after what it scans, states its own scope in a docblock, and holds its count in a named constant so a slice that legitimately adds a call site changes one line that says what it means.

**The scan is kept rather than deleted, and that is a decision.** It is the check that caught the third copy of the pair in issue #369, and a cheap mechanical backstop beside real behavioural evidence is worth its cost. What it may not do is stand in for the claim, or fail under a name that misdescribes the failure.

**Decision two. A publication assertion reads the seed list, not the call count.** #375 retargeted twenty spies from `invalidate` to `flush` and kept the local name `invalidate`, and ADR-081 priced that move while ADR-082 answered half of its worry correctly: after the split, `flush` is no longer the tick path, so a spy on it sees strictly less than it did. The surviving half is the drain, which publishes through `flush([])` when the deferral it replays carried no frame, so `toHaveBeenCalledTimes(1)` asserts "one publication of any origin" while the case around it says "one live write reached the graph once". No affected rig currently leaves a drain pending, which is why all twenty were green and why this is a test-quality finding and not a bug. It is still a case that can go red for a reason it is not about, or stay green because a drain supplied the publication the assertion wanted.

**Corrected in place, 2026-09-16.** The clause above said the drain publishes through `flush([])` without qualification, which stopped being true when ADR-088 made a frame-carrying deferral replay through `flushAtTick`. It is rewritten rather than marked superseded, which is the one exception the convention in [PR-WORKFLOW.md](./PR-WORKFLOW.md) names: the amendment below already answers this decision inside this same file, and a record must not be left disagreeing with itself. [Issue #418](https://github.com/chahyasantoso/motion5/issues/418).

Three answers in `packages/core/test/helpers/publication-spy.ts` own the distinction, so twenty cases share one reading of it rather than twenty copies of a filter: `publicationsFor(spy, seeds)` for a case whose seed list is the point, `statedPublications(spy)` for a case where the origin is the point and the exact list is not, and `drains(spy)` for the empty-seed publications only a scheduled drain makes. Exactly, and not by containment: a publication naming more nodes than the write did is a different event.

**The rename lands in the same change, and only in it.** #375 refused a rename on its own and that refusal was right in isolation, since churn in front of twenty assertions hides a slice. It stops being right once the scope of what the spy sees has actually changed, because then the old name is not a harmless holdover, it is a wrong claim about what the assertion covers. `publication` rather than `flush`, because `LV-5` already binds `flush` to a source region and one file should not spell one word two ways.

**Decision three. The twenty-first case proves the helpers.** #381 asks for one case showing that a scheduled drain cannot be mistaken for a write's own publication, and without it the helpers those twenty cases now depend on would themselves be unproven. `publication-origin.test.ts` drives a real deferral, runs the drain, and shows a bare count reading two where the write asked for one while each filtered answer still says the true thing.

**Decision four. `LV-15`'s explanation is rewritten around the path that exists, and its provenance is dropped rather than repaired.** The paragraph said the message reads `GraphRuntime is disposed.` because `#writeValues` ends at an inline `this.#graph.invalidate`. Every causal clause was false, in two stages: ADR-078 moved the value tier's publication so `#writeValues` ends at `#publishValue`, and #375 then deleted the member the sentence named, so a reader could not even find the thing being explained. The assertion itself was always correct.

The repair is not a rename. Substituting `flush` for `invalidate` would leave the sentence claiming an inline call in a member that has not made one for two records. The rewrite states the current owner and the current path, and it drops the `#288` and `#303` provenance: both corrected a lower layer reporting a disposal it merely noticed, and the claim here is that the owner refuses before the graph is asked anything, which is a different claim. Provenance that no longer explains the case is the same defect one size smaller.

**Consequences.** No source under `packages/core/src` is touched. No public surface, no behaviour, no diagnostic id and no rule id moves. Twenty spies are renamed and their count assertions are filtered; `LV-5` is renamed, loses eleven lexical assertions and gains a diagnostics case; `LV-15` gains a correct explanation. Three files are added: one helper and two test files. `LV-5`'s title changes, because "invalidates exactly once" names a verb this tier retired.

**Two cases are expected to be red before this change** and both for honest reasons: the diagnostics case in `LV-5`, because nothing asserted the recording behaviourally, and `publication-origin`, because the helpers it reads do not exist. The rest are green on both sides by construction, which is what a test-quality slice should look like: the assertions are strictly narrower, so nothing that passed for a good reason stops passing.

**Alternatives refused.** Deleting the mechanical scan outright: refused above, it caught a real third copy. Leaving the scan in `LV-5` and only renaming the locals: refused, because #379's finding is that the failure is illegible where it sits, and a rename-only change is what #381 explicitly forbids. Filtering by containment rather than exact match: refused, because a publication of three nodes would satisfy a case about one. Asserting the seed list at every one of the twenty sites: refused where the existing case never asserted it, because inventing a seed list the case never claimed is a new assertion smuggled in under a test-quality slice; `statedPublications` is the honest narrowing there, and it is the one that closes the drain hole. Writing the filter inline at each site: refused as twenty owners of one question, which is the ADR-076 defect in the test tree. Keeping `#288` and `#303` in `LV-15` for continuity: refused, because provenance is either an explanation or it is decoration.

**Amendment, 2026-09-16: a publication spy claims what a seed list proves, over the member it watches.** [Issue #404](https://github.com/chahyasantoso/motion5/issues/404) reads decision two back and finds the helper claiming two things its mechanism cannot see. `drains` promised "a scheduled drain, and nothing else", and `flush` is public while an empty seed list is a real publication, so a direct `flush([])` is indistinguishable from a replay; since ADR-088 a deferral that carried a frame replays through `flushAtTick`, which a spy on `flush` never sees at all. The answer is renamed `emptySeedPublications` and its docblock states what it filters. `statedPublications` keeps its behaviour and loses the same claim, which sat in its docblock too. Origin stays with `publication-origin.test.ts`, the one case that watches the scheduler callback, exactly where decision three put it. This is #379's finding one level down: a name that claims more than the mechanism can check.

`PublicationSpy` described any mock, because its call tuple was `readonly unknown[]`. A spy on `replaceGraph` was therefore structurally assignable, `seedsOf` answered `undefined` for every one of its calls, and a negative assertion such as `expect(statedPublications(wrongSpy)).toHaveLength(0)` passed while watching the wrong member, with several migrated cases holding exactly that spy in the same scope. The tuple now begins with `readonly string[]`, which is the lighter of the two options the issue offers and keeps the helper free of vitest generics. `seedsOf` is deleted rather than narrowed: a runtime guard answering a question the type now answers is a second owner of it, and its `undefined` branch is precisely what made the wrong member quiet.

Two cases carry it. The compile-time one is red before the change in the way a type case is red: without the tuple, the `@ts-expect-error` above a `replaceGraph` spy is an unused directive and the typecheck fails on it, and the same case accepts a `flush` spy so the refusal is about the call shape rather than about spies. The behavioural one is green on both sides and could not have been written under the old name without asserting something false, which is the honest shape of a rename: it pins a direct `flush([])` the answer counts and a frame-carrying drain it misses, in one case, so the scope left behind is proven rather than described.
