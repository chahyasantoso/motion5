# ADR-079: One publication owner, and a return value that means one thing

**Status:** Accepted, 2026-09-14

**Context.** [Issue #369](https://github.com/chahyasantoso/motion5/issues/369) collected the findings deferred from the review of [#368](https://github.com/chahyasantoso/motion5/pull/368), the value-tier batch. None was incorrect at the reviewed head and all were commentary on a design the review otherwise endorsed, which is why they were left out of a pull request that was already fixing red CI. Recorded together because four of the six are one question asked from four directions, and the question is who owns a publication.

The reviewed head had three copies of the same two statements: invalidate a seed list on the graph, then record the diagnostics the returned batch carries. `#flush` had them, `#invalidateOne` had them, and public `invalidate` had them. ADR-078 examined the first two and decided to keep them apart. It did not examine the third, which was left standing by reasoning inherited from the second. The reviewed head also had one member answering a value that meant two different things, and a rung whose name described the opposite of what it did.

**Decision.** Four things, and the first of them reverses a decision ADR-078 made three weeks of commits ago rather than quietly diverging from it.

**One: `#invalidateSeeds(nodeIds)` is the one place a seed list becomes a published, recorded batch.** `#flush`, `#invalidateOne` and public `invalidate` all end there. Each keeps its own preconditions and its own failure contract, which is precisely what ADR-071 refused to merge and is not what this does: ADR-071's objection was that combining `#flush` and `#invalidateOne` would give one member a context-dependent contract, and here the contracts stay in the members that own them while only the mechanics move down.

ADR-078 refused this exact spelling, on the ground that `LV-5` and `PK-17` read `#invalidateOne`'s declaration and assert the two statements are inside it, so moving them would turn two green cases red for no behavioural reason. Remeasured for this record: `PK-17` reads `#writeValues(`, for `if (involved)` and `validateTrackDefinition(rewritten`, and its subject is a malformed stop list refused before anything mutates. One case reads `#invalidateOne`, not two. Three test files read regions of this source at all and none of them addresses public `invalidate`. So the price is one re-addressed case rather than two, and the thing being bought is larger than ADR-078 could see, because the third copy had not been found yet. Two copies of two statements is a tidy-up; three copies of one question is the defect this project cares about most.

What the four records naming `#invalidateOne` name is unchanged. It is still the value tier's single-node flush, it still asserts runtime liveness so a disposed project is reported by the owner that decided rather than by the layer that noticed, and it still answers the actual batch. Its identity was never the two statements underneath those facts. `LV-5` is re-addressed rather than weakened and comes out strictly stronger: it now asserts that exactly one member in the whole file names the graph call at all, which is a claim its previous form could not make and which a second copy anywhere else would have satisfied.

**Two: `#flush` answers nothing, and the empty-seed decision gets its own owner.** It returned `undefined` both for a disposed runtime and for an empty seed list, and `values` collapsed the two into an empty batch with `??`. Nothing was wrong at that head, because `#assertLive()` ran immediately before it at every call site. That is safety by statement ordering, and the next call site that does not happen to be written in that order is where it stops holding. So `#flush` keeps one decision, that a disposed runtime is skipped rather than reported, and returns `void` as it did before the batch; `#publishSeeds(seeds)` owns what an empty list means and is what `values` publishes through and what `#flush` delegates to, so the empty-seed rule still has exactly one owner. `#publishSeeds` asserts liveness itself, which is what makes its answer unambiguous by construction: every batch it hands back is one it published or one that had nothing to publish. `values` keeps its own re-ask after the recipe, because that one answers issue #288's question rather than this one, and two side-effect-free field reads cannot disagree.

The general form, and the reason this is a record rather than a diff: a sentinel that collapses two conditions is deleted rather than documented. Two owners cost one member here, and a comment saying which of the two an `undefined` meant would have cost a reader every time.

**Three: public `invalidate` is not folded into `#flush`.** The follow-up asked whether it could be, since no test reads its declaration the way one reads `#invalidateOne`'s. The coupling is genuinely absent and the answer is still no, for a stronger reason than the one it was exempted by. `invalidate([])` publishes today and answers the batch that publication produced, while `#flush` skips an empty list, so folding would silently change a public answer. It would also make a public verb's return optional, which hands its callers a second failure contract to narrow rather than removing a duplicate. The duplication those two members really shared was the mechanics, and decision one takes it.

**Four: `#refuseValueReentrant` becomes `#refuseImmediateReentrant`.** The old name read as a refusal of value-verb reentrancy, which is the opposite of its job: it is the pair of conditions a value verb must pass in order to be allowed to join a batch. Both conditions are about the immediate family that every verb in this class belongs to, which is the vocabulary `immediateInTransaction` and the immediate-verb refusal cases already use, and it leaves `#refuseReentrant` defensible as the total rung that adds the batch condition. Five call sites, no behaviour, no diagnostic and no rule id.

**Also decided, and small.** `#teardown` clears `#valueSeeds` beside `#open`: both are open transaction state, that member claims that concept, and relying on `values`'s own `finally` to finish half of its stated job made the claim half true rather than harmless. Both value-batch factories drop `as PatchBatch` for an annotated local: the two functions whose entire job is producing a correctly shaped batch had opted out of the check that they do it, and the review's open question is answered by looking, since `PatchBatch` is four readonly members and a frozen literal satisfies it unaided. And `RA-170` drives `setKeyframe` through `#recompileKeyframes` inside a batch, which the review traced by hand and no run had ever executed, even though it is the call `apps/ik-playground/src/scroll-reach.ts` makes. **Refined by [ADR-094](./ADR-094-one-closed-value-per-runtime-decision.md), 2026-09-18.** The sentence about both value-batch factories names functions the tree no longer has: `value-batch.ts` is deleted, and `batchFor` in `report.ts` is the one builder of the four-field batch that three modules used to build, so what that sentence decided about two functions now has one place to hold. The decision stands and only its spelling moved.

**Consequences.** One member gains publication mechanics and three lose a copy of them. `#flush` loses a return type, which its three settlement callers already ignored. Seven private members own seven questions where five owned seven. No public surface moves: nothing is added, removed or renamed on an exported type or member, no refusal set changes, and every diagnostic and rule id is untouched, which is why this ships as a fix rather than as a tier.

This record amends ADR-078's first decision rather than editing it, and leaves ADR-069, ADR-070 and ADR-071 standing, because each of them names `#invalidateOne` for a property this change preserves. A reader arriving at ADR-078's `#invalidateSeeds` refusal should read it as accurate about the tree it measured and superseded by the measurement above.

**Alternatives refused.** Renaming `#invalidateOne` to the shared owner and deleting the single-node member: it has a caller-facing report contract that `#flush` must not have, and four records name it. Giving `#flush` a discriminated result so its two conditions stayed distinguishable inside one member: that is the two-meanings defect with more syntax, and the caller would still have to branch on a condition it cannot cause. Keeping the casts and asserting the batch shape in a test instead: a compiler already answers that question, and a test that re-answers it is a second owner of a check.
